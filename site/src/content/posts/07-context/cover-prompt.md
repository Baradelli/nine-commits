# Cover image prompt — Post 07

Used only if replacing the generated typographic cover (`cover.png`) with
generative art.

## Parameters

- Size: 1200 × 627 (set it on the request; no image model honours a ratio
  written into the sentence)
- Aspect: 1.91:1, the Open Graph card ratio the site's covers are built to

## Prompt

> A dark editorial illustration for a technical article titled "Context Is the
> Real Constraint". Central image: one very tall, very narrow empty vessel
> standing upright and running off the top of the frame, drawn as a thin
> outline. At its base a shallow band of liquid fills only the bottom fiftieth
> of it, and that band is the only solid colour in the picture. Beside it, a
> second vessel of exactly the same shape is filled to about half its height,
> and a single horizontal line cuts across it well below the surface, with
> everything above the line drawn as a faint dotted ghost — still present in
> the drawing, no longer part of the liquid. Flat vector style, limited palette
> of blueprint navy (#0b1e2d), cool off-white (#dce6ec) and a single violet
> accent (#6a46b8) used only on the filled band and on the cut line. Generous
> negative space on the left third for a title overlay. No text in the image.
> No numerals. No people. No robots, no glowing brains, no circuit board
> motifs, no funnels, no hourglasses.

## Why this image

Two things had to be in one picture, because the thesis has two halves and they
point in opposite directions.

The first half said one web search can fill the window. It does not: one search
and one page read is about two per cent of what this model will accept, and it
would take roughly forty-seven of them to fill it. The tall vessel with a
thumbnail of liquid at the bottom is that measurement, and it runs off the top
of the frame because you cannot draw the ratio and see both ends of it.

The second half is the cut. Compaction is not a tap or a filter; it is a line
drawn across a conversation with everything above it replaced by a sentence
about what used to be there. The ghosted portion is doing the work: the
material is not gone from the record — the trace still has every frame — it is
gone from the thing that is thinking. That distinction is the post's most
useful paragraph and it is the one a picture can carry.

The two vessels are the same shape on purpose. The experiment changed one
thing.

## Negative prompt

funnel, hourglass, sand timer, egg timer, water bottle, wine glass, measuring
jug with printed graduations, brain, glowing orb, neural network diagram,
circuit board, server rack, cloud icon, compression icon, zip file, folder,
scissors, garbage bin, broom, magnifying glass, search bar, browser window,
globe, world map, stock-photo businessman, lens flare, text, numerals,
watermark, three-dimensional rendering, gradient mesh

The negative list names the funnel and the hourglass because "context window"
and "compaction" are phrases a diffusion model draws as a narrowing tube, and a
funnel says the material comes out the other end. It does not. It names the
scissors and the bin because compaction is not deletion — a summary stands in
for what was removed — and a picture of throwing something away would
illustrate a claim this post does not make. It names the magnifying glass, the
browser window and the globe because the web search is this post's instrument
and not its subject; the post is about what happens to the context afterwards.
